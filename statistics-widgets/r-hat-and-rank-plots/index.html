R̂ — when do four chains agree?

Four chains that look alike are not enough — R̂ asks how alike. It compares the spread of the four chains' centres against the width of a single chain, and the rank plot in the middle is that same comparison as a picture.

What this shows

When a sampler has converged, every chain is drawing from the same posterior, so the chains should be interchangeable. R̂ turns that into a number. It compares W, the average variance within a chain, with b, the variance between the four chain means:

R̂ = √(1 − 1/N + b/W)

If the chains all sit on top of each other, b is nothing but sampling noise and R̂ ≈ 1. If they have drifted apart, b grows while W does not, and R̂ rises. The usual rule of thumb is to distrust anything above 1.01.

R̂ only ever looks at chain means, so on its own it is blind to four chains that share a centre and differ in width. The fix is to look twice. Fold the draws — replace each one by |θ − median| — and a wide chain acquires a large mean while a narrow one acquires a small one: a difference in spread has become a difference in location, which the same formula can then see. What gets reported is the larger of the two views. The view buttons switch between them, and all three panels follow.

Here the four chains are stand-ins: instead of running a sampler, each chain is N independent draws from a Normal whose centre and width you set. That is not what a real chain looks like, but it isolates exactly what R̂ can see.

How to use it

  • μ1–μ4 move the chains' centres apart. This is the only thing R̂ reacts to, so watch the √b ruler in step 3 grow.
  • σ1–σ4 set how wide each chain is. Widening every chain raises √W, which makes the same disagreement look smaller — R̂ is a ratio, not a distance.
  • N is the number of draws per chain. Raising it extends the same chains rather than redrawing them.
  • seed and new sample redraw everything. Same seed, same picture, for everyone who opens the link.
  • view switches all three panels between the draws and their folded version. The big number stays the reported R̂ — the larger of the two — while the rulers and the formula below it belong to whichever view you are looking at.
  • In step 2 each chain gets its own histogram of where its draws sit in the pooled ordering. The dashed line is what a flat histogram would be; the triangle on the baseline is that chain's mean rank, and the dotted line is where that mean rank would sit if the chain were a fair slice of the pool. The gap between triangle and dotted line is what feeds b.

Things to try

  • Start from converged and nudge μ4 up in steps of 0.05. R̂ passes 1.01 while the traces still look thoroughly mixed — the four centres only have to disagree by about a seventh of one chain's width to fail the test.
  • Now set every σ to 3 and repeat. The same offset in μ is forgiven, because √W is three times bigger. R̂ measures disagreement in units of the posterior's own width.
  • Load different scales and stay on the θ view. The rank plot is obviously wrong — the narrow chain bulges in the middle, the wide one piles up at both ends — and yet R̂(θ) reads 1.00, because the four means agree. Now press |θ − median|. The four chains separate, the rank histograms tilt, and the statistic climbs to about 1.28. Nothing about the formula changed; only what it was shown.
  • Go back to two modes and switch to the folded view. Now it is the folded number that reads 1.00, because folding throws away exactly the information that case depends on. Neither view is sufficient alone, which is why the reported value is the larger of the two.
  • Pull N down to 50 with all four chains identical and press new sample a dozen times. R̂ crosses 1.01 on roughly one draw in eight, from four chains that are genuinely the same. Short chains cannot be diagnosed.

Notes

  • The statistic here is the textbook Gelman–Rubin R̂, computed on the raw draws. The form shown is algebraically identical to the usual R̂ = √(V̂/W) with V̂ = ((N−1)/N)W + B/N and B = N/(M−1) Σ(θ‾m − θ‾)²; it is written with b = B/N so that every symbol is something on the screen.
  • The fold is taken about the median of all 4N pooled draws, and the folded view's densities are the corresponding folded Normals. Reporting the maximum over the two views follows Vehtari et al. (2021), where they are called the bulk and tail versions.
  • What PyMC reports is still a little more than this. ArviZ also splits each chain in half before doing any of it, so a chain that drifts is caught even when its overall mean is fine, and it replaces each draw by the normal score of its pooled rank first, which makes the statistic robust to heavy tails. Those two layers are deliberately left out here; what remains is the mechanism they are built on. They are why the number in az.summary will not match this one exactly. Note in particular that plain R̂ is not merely blind to drift but actively fooled by it: a drifting chain has an inflated within-chain variance, which pushes R̂ down.
  • R̂ is a random quantity, and with four identical chains it lands slightly below 1 about as often as slightly above. The widget reports what it computed rather than clipping at 1.
  • The rank plot is az.plot_rank in miniature: 20 bins over the ranks 1…4N, one histogram per chain, on a shared vertical scale.
  • Draws come from a seeded generator in the page, so a link reproduces the picture exactly, down to the individual draws. Everything is in the URL — copy it and everyone sees the same thing.
