Keeping your existing headline number

Recording a graded outcome doesn't cost you the single figure your board asks for. It does show you that the figure was never comparable to anyone else's.

Two programmes, one number

These two cohorts report exactly the same headline under the usual line, and are not the same at all. This is what a shared definition is for: not to stop you reporting a headline, but to make two programmes' headlines mean the same thing.

What follows from this

  • You keep your number. Record the grade, collapse it at the end. Every row in that table is one line of arithmetic away, including the one you already publish.
  • You gain a better one. A median time-to-placement, or a mean rung, is still a single figure a board can read — and it doesn't move when someone changes the cut-off.
  • Your history survives. Past cohorts can be re-scored onto the ladder from records you already hold, so the new series joins the old one instead of replacing it.
  • Cross-programme comparison needs this. Two programmes quoting different numbers may be doing equally well; two quoting the same number may not be. Without an agreed collapse rule, neither comparison can be made — see methodology.md §10.
The arithmetic, and how much to trust it

Headlines are cumulative counts over the buckets you set, divided by the cohort size. The "instead" figure is the mean rung for the ladder, and the median months-to-placement for the time version — read off the bucket in which the halfway person falls.

Ladder levels are those drafted in methodology.md §4.1 and are still open for revision; the argument here does not depend on where the rungs sit, only on there being more than two of them. For why the collapse loses information in the first place, see W4.

The time version's last bucket — not placed within two years — is censored rather than a failure: those people may still be placed later, and cohorts observed for different lengths of time are not directly comparable. That is a separate problem, handled in methodology.md §4.2.

The cohorts here are illustrative, not data from any programme.
