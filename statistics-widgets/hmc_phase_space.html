Mean accept prob averages min(1, e^(−ΔH)) over all proposals.

Mean e^(−ΔH) should converge to 1 for any reversible, volume-preserving integrator such as leapfrog, which makes it a check that the sampler is correct. Divergent trajectories count as 0.

Divergent counts trajectories whose energy error exceeded 1000. They are always rejected.

Keys: Space play/pause · → one proposal.

1 · Position space q

The banana target. The hatched region is energetically forbidden at the current energy H₀: you can only reach q where U(q) ≤ H₀. Its edge is where the particle would stop dead.

leapfrog path (one dot per step) current momentum p U(q) = H₀, the turning boundary samples (accepted states) accepted endpoint · ✕ rejected endpoint

2 · Momentum space p

The energy surface H = H₀ is a 3-D shell in 4-D. Above each position q it contains one circle of momenta, with radius r = √(2(H₀ − U(q))). Exact dynamics keeps |p| = r as q moves, so |p| grows as q falls into the banana and shrinks as q nears the turning boundary. Leapfrog keeps it close, but not exactly equal.

p along the trajectory dashed rings: |p| = 1, 2, 3 of the N(0, I) momentum distribution

3 · Energy plane (U, K)

Each 4-D state collapsed to its potential and kinetic energy. Energy levels are diagonals. Resampling p is a vertical jump, since U is unchanged. Leapfrog then slides along a diagonal, trading U for K.

Shading is the joint density e^(−(U+K)). Margins show the Exp(1) densities of K (left) and U (bottom); the bars are the U values of the samples.

4 · Energy error ΔH = H − H₀

Top: a zoomed-in view of how far leapfrog strays from H₀ at each step. It oscillates instead of drifting, because leapfrog is symplectic. A uniform u is drawn up front, and the proposal is rejected if the final ΔH > −log u. Grey curves are recent trajectories.

Bottom: final ΔH over all proposals. The dashed curve is the rejection probability for each ΔH. Values beyond the axis range are counted in the edge bins.

What to look for

  1. Sticking to the energy surface. In panel 2 the momentum magnitude |p| stays close to √(2(H₀ − U(q))), the radius of the energy-surface circle over the current q. |p| grows as q falls into the banana, where potential turns into kinetic energy, and shrinks as q climbs toward the hatched boundary. In panel 3 the path slides along the orange diagonal.
  2. Not quite sticking. Leapfrog is a discrete approximation, so H wobbles slightly (panel 4). At Δt = 0.1 the wobble is usually around 10⁻², far below the typical rejection threshold −log u ≈ 1. That is why HMC accepts nearly everything, while each proposal still travels a long way.
  3. Where the rejections come from. The proposal is rejected with probability 1 − e^(−ΔH) when ΔH > 0. Increase Δt to 0.3–0.4: ΔH grows and red appears in the histogram. From about 0.4, trajectories that reach the banana's tips, where the curvature is much higher, start to diverge outright: leapfrog is only stable when Δt is small relative to the local curvature.
  4. A neat fact about this target. The banana map y = (q₁/a, a·q₂ + ab(q₁² + a²)) has unit Jacobian, so under the target U(q) is exactly Exp(1), just like K(p) for p ~ N(0, I). In the energy plane the joint density is therefore e^(−(U+K)). It is constant along each energy line, and the shading shows it exactly. The bars under panel 3 (U of the samples) should converge to the Exp(1) curve.
