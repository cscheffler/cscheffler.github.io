The Normal-Inverse-Gamma — m, s² ~ NIG(μ0, ν0, α0, β0)

A joint distribution over a mean and a variance at the same time. It is not two independent beliefs side by side: the spread of m is tied to s², which is what gives the contours their funnel shape.

What this shows

The Normal-Inverse-Gamma is the conjugate prior for a Normal likelihood when both the mean and the variance are unknown. It is a distribution over a pair, and it factors as

s² ~ Inv-Gamma(α0, β0),   m | s² ~ Normal(μ0, s²/ν0)

Read that second line carefully: the variance of m is s²/ν0, so it depends on s². High up the plot, where the variance is large, m is loosely pinned; low down it is tightly pinned. That is the funnel, and it is the whole point of the distribution — how sure you are about the mean depends on how noisy you think the data are.

The strip above shows the marginal of m once s² is integrated out: a Student t with 2α0 degrees of freedom, centred on μ0. The strip on the right shows the marginal of s², which is the Inverse-Gamma itself.

How to use it

  • μ0 slides the whole thing left and right. Nothing about its shape changes.
  • ν0 is how many pseudo-observations the prior on the mean is worth. Raising it squeezes the funnel horizontally without touching the marginal of s².
  • α0 and β0 shape the variance. β0/(α0+1) is the most likely s²; β0/(α0−1) is its mean, when α0 > 1. Raising both together at a fixed ratio concentrates the variance without moving it.
  • auto-scale axes — on by default. Switch it off to freeze the current window, which is what to do before changing a parameter if you want to compare like with like. The frozen window travels with the URL.
  • colour map — cividis by default; viridis is there to compare. See the notes.
  • contours enclose 50%, 80% and 95% of the probability mass, innermost first, drawn with the thickest line for the innermost so they are told apart without relying on position alone.

Things to try

  • Start at the defaults and look at the shape. It is not an ellipse: it leans and flares upward. Slice it at a low s² and m is confined to a narrow band; slice it near the top and the same m range is wide open.
  • Turn auto-scale axes off, then raise ν0 from 1 to 10. The funnel narrows sharply while the strip on the right does not move at all. ν0 is information about the mean only.
  • Now put ν0 back and raise α0 and β0 together, say (3,2) → (30,20) → (300,200) by typing in the boxes. The variance concentrates on 2/3, the funnel stops flaring, and the top strip approaches a Normal — a t with many degrees of freedom.
  • Drop α0 to 0.5. The tail on s² becomes very heavy, the window has to open up to hold the mass, and the t on top gets fat tails. This is what a vague prior on the variance actually looks like.
  • Compare the two colour maps on the same picture. If you have normal colour vision they look different; the point of cividis is that they do not look meaningfully different to someone with red-green colour blindness.

Notes

  • The density is p(m, s²) ∝ (s²)−α0−3/2 exp[−(2β0 + ν0(m−μ0)²) / (2s²)], evaluated in log space with a Lanczos log Γ. Both marginals were checked against numerical integration of the joint and agree to about 1 part in 1013.
  • Why cividis rather than viridis. Both are perceptually uniform and monotonic in lightness, so both survive being printed in greyscale. Cividis goes further: it was built specifically so that a viewer with deuteranopia or protanopia — the common forms of red-green colour blindness, around 1 in 12 men — sees essentially the same image as a viewer without. Viridis is good on this; cividis is optimised for it, which is why it is the default here. Viridis is one control away if you prefer it.
  • Colour runs from zero to the largest density in view on a square-root scale. A linear ramp, which is what imshow uses by default, leaves almost the whole panel one flat colour here: the window is chosen to hold nearly all the mass, so most of it sits far below the peak. The equivalent in matplotlib is norm=PowerNorm(0.5).
  • The window is chosen to hold about 99% of the mass: s² runs from 0 to the 99th percentile of the Inverse-Gamma, and m spans μ0 ± 3.2 conditional standard deviations taken at the 90th percentile of s². The exact fraction held is reported under the plot, and the contour levels are found from the density values on the rendered grid, so a contour is only drawn when the window really does contain that much mass.
  • The URL updates as you change the inputs, so you can copy it from the address bar and share an exact configuration, frozen window included.
