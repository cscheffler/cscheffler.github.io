What this shows

R̂ compares the variance between chain means with the average variance within a chain: R̂ = √(1 − 1/N + b/W). That ratio is the engine, and it never changes here. What changes is what you feed it. The modern statistic (Vehtari, Gelman, Simpson, Carpenter and Bürkner, 2021) feeds it three times over, and each layer is there because the previous one cannot see something:

  • Split. Each chain is cut into its first and second half, giving eight half-chains instead of four chains. A chain that drifts steadily has the right overall mean, so the unsplit statistic is blind to it — worse than blind, in fact: the drift inflates that chain's own variance, so W grows and R̂ is pushed below 1. Split, the two halves disagree and it shows.
  • Rank-normalise. Every draw is replaced by the normal score of its rank among all the pooled draws. b and W are variances, so they need the posterior to have a finite one; with heavy tails W is enormous and swamps a real disagreement between centres. Ranks have no tails, so the disagreement survives.
  • Fold. Everything above looks only at means, so four chains sharing a centre and differing in width sail through. Replacing each draw by |θ − median| turns a difference in spread into a difference in location.

The reported value is the larger of the rank-normalised statistic (the bulk) and the folded one (the tail). Each is blind to what the other catches.

How to use it

  • μ, σ and drift set each chain's centre, width, and how far it slides from start to finish. Drift is centred, so it leaves the chain's overall mean alone — which is the whole point of it.
  • tails replaces the Normal draws with Student-t on the same underlying randomness. At t2 the variance is infinite; at t1 (Cauchy) so is the mean.
  • stage moves the first two panels along the pipeline. Stage 1 is the draws as you set them; stage 2 cuts them in half; stage 3 is the rank-normalised values the bulk statistic is computed from; stage 4 is the folded ones behind the tail statistic.
  • The ladder in step 3 gives the statistic at every stage at once, so you can see which layer first notices the problem. Clicking a row selects that stage.
  • seed and new sample redraw everything reproducibly; the whole state is in the URL, so a link reproduces the picture exactly.

Things to try

  • drifting chain. Chain 4 slides steadily across the run. Stage 1 reads 1.000 — the unsplit statistic cannot see it at all. Switch to stage 2 and watch the single mean line become two, far apart, and the ladder jump to 1.073. This is the case splitting exists for.
  • heavy tails. Chain 4 is centred a full 1.5σ away from the others, and the draws are Cauchy. Stages 1 and 2 both read 0.999: the tails make W so large that a real, large disagreement vanishes into it. Stage 3 rank-normalises and reads 1.048. Press new sample a few times — the failure is not a fluke of one seed.
  • different scales. Only the tail row moves (1.218). Every other row is looking at means, and the means agree exactly.
  • two modes. Watch the number come down the ladder: 3.08 raw, 2.87 split, 1.73 rank-normalised. Rank-normalising bounds the statistic, because normal scores are bounded; the diagnosis is unchanged, but the size of the number is not comparable to the raw one.
  • Sit on stage 2, then switch to stage 3 and watch panel 2. The rank plot does not move at all. Rank-normalising is a monotone transform, so it cannot change any rank — it changes the values that b and W are computed from, and nothing else.

Notes

  • The pipeline is exactly ArviZ's rhat(method="rank"), the default that az.summary reports: split each chain at n // 2 (an odd middle draw is dropped, as ArviZ does), rank all the pooled values with ties averaged, map them through Φ−1((r − 3/8)/(S + 1/4)) — Blom's offset — and take the between/within ratio; then repeat on |θ − median| where the median is taken over the split array; report the maximum.
  • Checked against ArviZ 1.3.0 on eight configurations spanning all six presets: the reported value agrees to within 3×10−8, which is the residual of this page's Φ−1 against SciPy's. The split-only row agrees with rhat(method="split") to machine precision.
  • Student-t draws are built as z / √(χ²ν/ν) from a fixed pool of normals, so changing ν reshapes the same underlying randomness instead of reshuffling it.
  • The vertical axis in panel 1 is cut at the 0.5% and 99.5% quantiles of the values on screen; with Cauchy draws a handful of points land far outside, and the count is reported under the plot. Nothing is excluded from the arithmetic — only from the picture.
  • Still not the whole story. The paper also proposes localised versions (R̂ computed on quantile indicators) and pairs the statistic with bulk and tail effective sample sizes; neither is here. R̂ also says nothing about how many effective draws you have — a chain can pass at 1.00 and still be far too short to use.
